RL optimizes the mean. TailRL shapes the tail.

From a continuous reward to a likelihood

Step 1

Every reward level asks a question

For each level, ask whether a rollout's reward exceeds it. Better rollouts pass more levels.

Step 2

Test the levels independently

Assign each level a fresh rollout. The policy passes the quality audit only if every rollout clears its assigned level. Independent rollouts keep the levels as separate likelihood factors.

Step 3

Average the log-likelihood

Refining the audit should not change the quantity being measured, so we use the log-likelihood per level. In the continuum, this becomes TailRL.

$J_{\mathrm{TailRL}}(\theta;x) = \displaystyle\int_0^1 \log p_\theta(x,\tau)\,d\tau$

Here $p_\theta(x,\tau)$ is the probability that one rollout exceeds reward level $\tau$.

TailRL maximizes the average log-probability of reaching the quality levels implied by a continuous verifier.

On binary rewards, every nontrivial level asks the same question: did the rollout succeed? TailRL therefore reduces exactly to MaxRL. Related project: MaxRL for binary rewards →

Show the finite audit and derivation

For $L$ equally spaced levels $\tau_\ell=(\ell-1)/L$, draw independent rollouts $z_1,\ldots,z_L\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ and let $E_L$ be the event that every one clears its assigned level. Independence factorizes the likelihood:

$E_L := \displaystyle\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{L}\left\{r(x,z_\ell)>\tau_\ell\right\}, \qquad \Pr_\theta(E_L\mid x) = \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} p_\theta(x,\tau_\ell)$

The number of levels sets only the resolution of the audit. Requiring the objective to be invariant to that resolution, while agreeing with the ordinary log-likelihood at $L=1$, fixes the normalization at $1/L$. The result is a Riemann sum, and letting $L\to\infty$ gives the population objective:

$\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L}\log\Pr_\theta(E_L\mid x) = \lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\log p_\theta(x,\tau_\ell) = \int_0^1 \log p_\theta(x,\tau)\,d\tau$

The audit defines the population likelihood. It is not a sampling procedure: the finite-rollout estimator below does not draw one rollout per level.

Sampling compute changes the objective

Truncating the logarithm gives a compute-indexed family. Its first member is expected-reward RL; increasing the order moves the objective toward the full tail likelihood.

$\nabla_\theta J_{\mathrm{TailRL}}^{(T)}(\theta;x) = \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\frac{1}{k}\,\nabla_\theta\,\text{Best-of-}k(\theta;x)$

TailRL is a harmonic mixture of Best-of-$k$ objectives across inference budgets. The weights come from the logarithm; they are not tuned.

Show the harmonic derivation

Truncating the Mercator series $\log p = -\sum_{k\ge1}(1-p)^k/k$ after $T$ terms and integrating over the reward level gives

$J_{\mathrm{TailRL}}^{(T)}(\theta;x) := -\displaystyle\int_0^1\sum_{k=1}^{T}\frac{\left(1-p_\theta(x,\tau)\right)^k}{k}\,d\tau$

By the layer-cake identity, $\text{Best-of-}k(\theta;x)=\int_0^1\left[1-(1-p_\theta(x,\tau))^k\right]d\tau$. Substituting gives $J_{\mathrm{TailRL}}^{(T)} = \sum_{k=1}^{T}\left(\text{Best-of-}k - 1\right)/k$, whose gradient is the identity above. At $T=1$ this is expected-reward RL; as $T\to\infty$ it is the tail likelihood.

Rare reward levels should not vanish into the mean

Expected reward and TailRL can see the same rollouts and make different updates. The difference is how much each reward level matters.

Expected reward / RLOO

One weight for every reward level. Rare levels matter only in proportion to how often the policy reaches them.

level weight $= 1$
GRPO

One detached scale for the whole input update. GRPO changes how much an input matters, but not the relative importance of reward levels within it.

input scale $= 1/\sigma(x)$
TailRL

A different weight at every reward level. Levels the policy reaches rarely receive more influence.

level weight $= 1/p(x,\tau)$

Rare, exceptional rollouts remain visible to the update instead of disappearing into the mean.

A new objective, not a new training stack

TailRL uses the same rollout groups as GRPO and RLOO. Sort the rewards, divide each reward gap among the rollouts that reach it, mean-center the resulting weights, and use them as advantages.

No critic No tail-probability model No new hyperparameter One sort + linear passes

In a group-based RL pipeline, only the advantage computation changes.

Show the estimator theorem

Given $N$ i.i.d. rollouts $z_1,\ldots,z_N\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ with rewards $r_i:=r(x,z_i)$ and score $S(x,z):=\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(z\mid x)$, the estimator

$g^{(N)}_{\mathrm{TailRL}}(x) := \displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega(r_i)\,S(x,z_i), \qquad \omega(r_i) := \int_0^{r_i}\frac{d\tau}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{r_j>\tau\}}$

is unbiased for $\nabla_\theta J^{(N)}_{\mathrm{TailRL}}(\theta;x)$. The denominator counts the survivors at each level, so a level cleared by one rollout out of $N$ is worth $N$ times a level cleared by all.

The integrand is piecewise constant, so the weight has a closed form as a gap-over-survivors sum over the sorted rewards $r_{(1)}\le\cdots\le r_{(N)}$, with $r_{(0)}:=0$:

$\omega\!\left(r_{(i)}\right) = \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{r_{(k)}-r_{(k-1)}}{N-k+1}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega\!\left(r_{(i)}\right) = \max_{j} r_j$

The weights are nonnegative and sum to the largest reward in the group, so the best reward observed controls the scale of the update. Without centering the estimator targets the order-$N$ objective; with the mean-centering used in training it targets order $N-1$.

TailRL moves the sampling-compute frontier

Across every tested model and task, TailRL improves the performance-versus-sampling-compute frontier over expected-reward baselines. The largest gaps appear when exceptional outcomes are rare or when a safe, moderate shortcut dominates the mean.

Does the finite estimator approach the exact objective?

ImageNet localization

The categorical policy is tractable enough to optimize population TailRL exactly. Finite-rollout TailRL approaches that reference as $N$ grows, and TailRL at $N=16$ already exceeds GRPO and RLOO trained with $N=1024$.

Using only scalar IoU rewards, population TailRL also matches or exceeds task-specific coordinate supervision.

Task details

A ResNet-50 backbone feeds four categorical heads that sample a bounding box centre, width and height. The reward is the box's IoU with the ground truth. Because the policy is a tractable categorical distribution over a finite box set, the exact policy-induced reward distribution, and so the population objective, can be computed directly. Metrics are CorLoc@$\delta$, mean IoU and Best-of-$k$ IoU on held-out validation images.

Can RL learn when exceptional outcomes are almost absent?

Text-maze navigation

We vary the initial shortest-path success rate from about $1\%$ to $0.01\%$. TailRL remains reliable in the rare-success regime, where RLOO stays at zero and GRPO becomes seed-dependent.

TailRL separates exactly where the high-reward tail becomes hard to observe.

Do similar one-sample policies scale differently with more inference?

GUI grounding with 3B and 7B vision-language models

TailRL and RLOO have similar Pass@$1$, but their curves separate as more samples are drawn. TailRL matches RLOO's Pass@$1024$ with $8$ rollouts at 3B and $4$ at 7B: $128\times$ and $256\times$ less inference sampling.

Single-sample accuracy hides how much useful behavior remains recoverable from the policy.

Can the objective escape a safe but useless shortcut?

Code runtime optimization

Copying the input is always correct and earns reward $1$. GRPO and RLOO collapse to this shortcut; TailRL keeps producing risky rewrites and reaches $7.7\times$ Best-of-$1024$ speedup on held-out problems, versus below $1\times$ for both baselines.

Expected reward settles for the common moderate outcome. TailRL preserves the rare behavior needed to improve it.

Expected-reward training fails in two ways: it overlooks rare good outcomes, or it collapses to a frequent moderate one. TailRL addresses both by keeping rarely reached reward levels influential.

One objective. The same rollout pipeline. A better tail.
